# Research status

Updated October 26, 2026.

The independent Cenorya review concerns adult wearable physiological sensing in relation to reference-measured blood pressure. Its protocol is a draft and is not yet approved or registered. Formal comprehensive screening and PRISMA counts have not begun. The planned approach follows JBI scoping-review methodology, with PRISMA-ScR and PRISMA-S reporting.

## Questions guiding the work

1. Does a study estimate BP at the same time as the input, track change, classify current status, or predict a genuinely later event?
2. What input-to-reference gap is specified, and what information is available at the decision time?
3. Were measurement prompts actually evaluated, including missed elevations, false prompts and burden?
4. Who has usable signal and reference measurements, and who is excluded?

Existing reviews cover much of the technology and validation landscape. A closely related project also studies near-term BP modeling from wearable and self-report information. These are important sources of prior work; they prevent treating the broad prediction idea as new. A distinct Cenorya contribution has not yet been established.

## Evidence and equity

Race and ethnicity do not substitute for measured skin pigmentation or perfusion. Future evaluations should make signal rejection, missingness, reference availability and subgroup performance explicit. No alert during missing or unusable data must not be interpreted as normal BP.

The intended maternal focus does not make findings in other adult populations transferable without evaluation. Maternal-health review work and Postpartum Monitor remain separately governed.

## Research transparency

Research records are dated and append-only. Exploratory searches, formally executed database searches, screening decisions and engineering simulations are distinguished. AI-assisted literature judgments require investigator review. Danielle Holmes is the principal investigator and final decision-maker.
